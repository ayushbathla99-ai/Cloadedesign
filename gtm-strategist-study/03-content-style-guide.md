# 03 — Content Style Guide: The GTM Strategist Voice, Decoded

This guide reverse-engineers how Maja Voje writes, so you can **replicate the mechanics in your own voice** on LinkedIn. Short quotes are included for analysis only. Don't reuse her lines, her coined terms (ECP, GTM Power Hour, Market-Problem Map…), or her stories. Use the *patterns*.

---

## 1. Voice DNA (the 8 traits)

| # | Trait | What it sounds like | How to do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trench-first authority** | "I was working with a team last week…", "In June, I trained 35 startup teams. 31 of them were already doing some form of outreach." | Open with something *you did recently*, with a number. Never open with a definition. |
| 2 | **Warm mentor, not guru** | "Dear GTM Strategist!", "I get it. I really do.", "You are normal. But you should do it in December." | Validate the reader's struggle before correcting it. |
| 3 | **Blunt about mistakes** | "Never put the word *growth hacking* in your pitch deck.", "The solution is never to do more, but to go back to the basics." | One sentence of uncomfortable truth per section. |
| 4 | **Anti-fluff, anti-guru** | "Hi Joe ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐, CEO who thinks your software is excellent.", mocking "I vibe-coded LinkedIn this weekend" clickbait | Name the cringe, then do better. Positions you as the sane adult in the feed. |
| 5 | **Vulnerable founder** | "I felt like a piece of shit", "5 Mistakes I Did with my GTM Strategist Launch", "retention has been a pain in the arse" | Share your own failures with numbers. That earns the right to prescribe. |
| 6 | **Playful, lightly irreverent** | 🤠 "duh", "heck", "Moby Dicks 🐳", "a crocodile that eats 95% of innovations 🐊", "Ladies, gentlemen, and nerds of all AI religions: Let's Grok!" | One joke or metaphor per ~400 words. Keep it short, never at the reader's expense. |
| 7 | **Data-anchored** | "I analyzed 199 pieces of LinkedIn content so you don't have to", "195 B2B software companies opened up…" | Every claim gets a number, source, or sample size. Show the sample before the insight. |
| 8 | **Action-obsessed** | "Less talking - more doing 💪", "Do less. Do it better.", "you were probably just 50 focused emails away" | End sections with what to *do*, not what to *think*. |

**Her recurring vocabulary** (adopt the spirit and pick your own equivalents): *mission-critical, radical focus, leverage, in the trenches, battle-tested, no-fluff, hands-on, the nitty-gritty, v01, ICP-first, moats, unfair advantage, hot take ♨️, spicy.*

## 2. Hook library: 12 opening patterns found in the corpus

Each pattern below includes a *new* example written for your niche (AI-led growth for SaaS, agencies, and service businesses).

| # | Pattern | Her structure | Your version (original) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Client-Call Replay** | Founder says X → "Me: …" → "And there it is." | *Founder: "We want 50 AI agents running our marketing." Me: "Great. What's the one workflow your best marketer repeats every week?" Silence. And there it is.* |
| 2 | **The Blunt Rule** | Bold one-line command, then "It is not X, but…" | ***Never ask an AI agent to "do marketing."** It's not that it can't. It's that you haven't told it what marketing means for you.* |
| 3 | **The Counted Observation** | "This month I [did N things]. [M] of them had [problem]." | *This quarter I audited 22 "AI-powered" marketing stacks. 19 of them were ChatGPT tabs with a Notion page.* |
| 4 | **The Fear Name-Check** | List the doom headlines → "😴 I am getting bored…" | *"AI killed SEO." "Outbound is dead." "LinkedIn reach is down 50%." Meanwhile my clients' pipeline is up. Here's what's different.* |
| 5 | **The Paradox** | "Everyone is X – but few are Y." | *Everyone is "using AI" for content. Almost nobody is using it to decide **what** content to make.* |
| 6 | **The Confession** | "I have a confession to make…" / "I've never told you this, but…" | *I've never told you this, but my first agent workflow cost me a client.* |
| 7 | **The Cringe Parody** | Quote three cringey LinkedIn lines → "Let's do better." | *"I replaced my team with AI." "This prompt is worth $10K." "Comment AGENT for the template." Let's do better.* |
| 8 | **The Research Flex** | "I analyzed N [things] so you don't have to." | *I took apart 40 AI-native SaaS landing pages so you don't have to.* |
| 9 | **The Personal Milestone + Gratitude** | Share a number → thank the reader → "So, would it make sense for you to…?" | *We crossed [X] subscribers this month. Thank you. Here's the exact system, so you can build yours.* |
| 10 | **The Quote Pivot** | Famous quote → reframe to GTM | *Kotler said pricing is the only part of the marketing mix that makes money. In 2026, distribution is the only part that can't be copied.* |
| 11 | **The Vivid Scene** | A moment with sensory detail → the lesson | *The whole office gathered around my screen as I hit send on my first 5,000-email campaign…* |
| 12 | **The Stakes Stack** | ☑ 3–4 market facts proving it's harder than ever → "Go-to-market is your last true advantage." | *☑ Building is cheaper than ever. ☑ Copying is faster than ever. ☑ CAC is up. ☑ Attention is down. Distribution is the moat.* |

**Rules for the first 3 lines on LinkedIn** (they show before "…see more"):
- Line 1: tension or a number. No greeting.
- Line 2: a short reaction or twist ("And there it is." / "Wait, what?" / "Here's the kicker:").
- Line 3: the promise or the "why now".

## 3. Article architecture (the master skeleton)

```
TITLE: [Number/Named framework] + [Outcome] + [Constraint or proof]
SUBTITLE: one line of proof or a "free template inside"

1. HOOK (60–120 words): pattern from §2
2. WHY NOW (50–100 words): market shift + 1 stat
3. CREDIBILITY (1–3 lines): "I've done this with N teams" / guest bio / data sample
4. PROMISE BLOCK: "In this article, you'll learn:" + 3–5 bold bullets
5. BODY: 3–7 modules, each = 
      ## Lesson heading (claim, not topic)
      Short explanation (3–6 short paragraphs)
      → Framework / visual / table
      → Example (real, with numbers)
      → "Do this:" action or template
6. WHERE I STAND: your verdict, 2–4 sentences, takes a side
7. REFLECTIVE QUESTION: ask the reader to self-diagnose
8. P.S.: next asset / event / resource
9. RESOURCE FOOTER (optional on LinkedIn): 2–3 links to your assets
```

**Length targets for LinkedIn articles/newsletter:** 900–1,800 words. Her Substack runs 2,000–3,000 words, but LinkedIn readers drop off sooner. Keep the skeleton and cut module count to 3–5.

## 5. Formatting signatures

- **Short paragraphs.** 1–3 sentences. Frequent one-line paragraphs for rhythm ("Such stories are outliers.").
- **Bold the claim**, not the decoration. Bold is used for the takeaway inside a sentence.
- **Bulleted promise blocks** with the key noun in bold.
- **Checkmark lists** (☑ / ✔️) for "stakes" and "what you need".
- **Tables** for comparisons, benchmarks, and stacks (era 3 especially).
- **Arrows (→)** for rapid-fire insights in hands-on posts.
- **Pull quotes** (`> “Features are easy to copy. Trust isn't.”`) from guests and data.
- **Emoji as punctuation, not decoration**: 🤠 (playful rebel), 🎯 (focus), ♨️ (hot take), 🎁 (freebie), 🙏 (thanks), 💪 (action). Max 3–5 per article.
- **Image captions** that explain the visual in one line.
- **"Pro tip:"** and **"Fair advice:"** callouts.
- **Parenthetical asides** for personality: "(duh)", "(full disclosure: …)", "(read: significant churn)".

## 6. Rhetorical moves to replicate

1. **Myth → Reality flip.** "You'll be tempted into thinking you have a pricing problem… You most likely have a product or segment problem."
2. **Diagnosis before prescription.** "Do You Have an Acquisition Problem or a Sales Problem?", "Death valley" fit tests. Let readers self-qualify.
3. **Two-sided honesty.** Show the downside ("Obviously, it comes with downsides too…", "🚨 Note that this tactic is not innocent").
4. **Full disclosure on sponsors and conflicts.** "Full disclosure: Cello sells referral software, and I'd still rather you launch nothing than…". This builds trust.
5. **"Where I stand"**. Always take a position at the end.
6. **Borrowed authority, attributed precisely.** Name, title, company, and *why* they're credible ("He lives with every decision he makes").
7. **Hand-over device.** "Over to Kyle." The guest gets a clean stage.
8. **Reader-as-hero framing.** "You are my why", "Let's get some ICP-fit demos, people!"
9. **Self-diagnostic question close.** "If you just ran the fit test in your head, which of the three conditions are you missing?"

## 7. Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Open with a recent, specific, numbered moment | Open with "In today's fast-paced world…" or a definition |
| Name your framework and draw it | List 15 generic tips |
| Give one usable artifact per article | Gate everything behind "comment X" |
| Show your sample size and the downside | Claim "this will 10x your pipeline" without proof |
| Write like a mentor on a call | Write like a press release or a guru |
| End with your verdict + a question | End with "What do you think? 👇" alone |
| Keep the promo in the P.S. | Make the promo the post (her lowest-engagement posts are promos) |
