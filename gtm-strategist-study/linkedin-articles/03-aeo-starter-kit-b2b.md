# How to Get Recommended by ChatGPT: The AEO Starter Kit for B2B

*The Answer Audit: 5 steps to find out what AI says about you (and change it), in one afternoon*

---

"How is AI search changing SEO, and what should we actually do about it?"

It's the question I get most from founders right now. Usually right after they've typed their own category into ChatGPT and watched it recommend three competitors and not them.

That moment stings. I've watched it happen live on [N] client calls this quarter.

Here's the uncomfortable part: your buyers are having that exact moment too, just without you in the room. They ask an AI assistant for a shortlist, and if you're not in the answer, you're not on the list.

The good news: this is still early. Most B2B companies haven't done the basics yet, so the basics are still a real advantage.

**In this article, you'll learn:**

- **The Answer Audit**: a 5-step process to see how AI engines currently describe your category and your brand
- **Where AI answers actually come from**, and why your blog alone won't fix this
- **A copy-paste tracking sheet** and the prompts I use with clients

No theory dump. Let's get our hands dirty.

---

## First, the mental model: you're optimizing for being *cited*, not *clicked*

Classic SEO asked: *"Can I rank on page one?"*

AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) asks: *"When an AI answers my buyer's question, am I part of the answer, described the way I want?"*

Three things change:

| | Classic SEO | AEO |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | A keyword | A question / prompt a buyer would ask |
| **Win** | A click to your page | A mention + an accurate description in the answer |
| **Signals** | Your pages + backlinks | Your pages **plus what everyone else says about you**: reviews, comparisons, communities, listicles, docs |

That last row is the one most teams miss. AI engines don't only read you. They read what others say about you.

---

## Step 1: Write the questions your buyers actually ask

Not keywords. Questions.

Pull them from places where buyers use their own words:

- sales call transcripts ("we're looking for something that…")
- support tickets and onboarding calls
- "alternatives to [competitor]" style searches
- Reddit and community threads in your space

Aim for **20–30 prompts** across three intents:

| Intent | Example prompt |
|---|---|
| **Category** | "What are the best [category] tools for a 50-person B2B SaaS company?" |
| **Problem** | "How do I [job-to-be-done] without [common pain]?" |
| **Comparison** | "[Competitor] vs alternatives for [use case]" |

**Pro tip:** feed 10 call transcripts to Claude and ask: *"List every question the prospect asked or implied before buying, in their own words."* That gives you more real buyer language than a keyword tool will.

---

## Step 2: Run the audit across engines

Run every prompt in **ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI results**. Use a fresh session (logged out or incognito where possible) so your own history doesn't skew the results.

For each answer, log:

- Are we mentioned? (Y/N)
- Position in the list
- **How** are we described? Is it accurate?
- Which competitors appear?
- Which **sources** are cited? (This is the gold.)

| Prompt | Engine | Mentioned? | Position | Description accurate? | Competitors named | Sources cited |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Best [category] for…" | ChatGPT | N | – | – | A, B, C | G2, [blog], Reddit |

Doing this by hand for 25 prompts × 4 engines takes an afternoon. Once you've done it, an agent can rerun it weekly (more on that in Step 5). If you'd rather use a tool, AI-visibility trackers now exist in most major SEO suites.

---

## Step 3: Map the sources, because that's where the work is

Look at the **Sources cited** column. You'll usually see a pattern:

- **Review sites** (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius)
- **"Best X tools" listicles** on third-party blogs
- **Community threads** (Reddit, niche Slack/Discord recaps)
- **Comparison pages** (often your competitors' own "[them] vs [you]" pages 😬)
- **Docs and help centers** with clear, factual answers

That list is your AEO to-do list. If AI engines keep citing a listicle you're not in, the fix isn't another blog post on your site. It's getting into that listicle, or publishing something more useful that sources start citing instead.

**The mistake:** treating AEO as a website-only project. A big part of it is distribution and PR.

---

## Step 4: Fix how you're described

Now make it easy for any engine (and any human) to describe you correctly.

1. **One canonical positioning sentence.** "[Brand] is a [category] for [ICP] that [outcome], unlike [alternative]." Use it word-for-word on your homepage, About page, LinkedIn page, review profiles, and directory listings. Consistency beats cleverness here.
2. **Publish your own comparison pages.** Write honest "[You] vs [Competitor]" and "Best [category] for [use case]" pages. Include where you *aren't* the best fit. Honest pages get cited. Salesy pages get ignored.
3. **Answer the questions directly.** For each buyer prompt from Step 1, make sure there's a clear, quotable answer somewhere on your site: FAQ, docs, or a guide. Lead with the answer, then explain.
4. **Ship proof others can cite.** Original data, benchmarks, or a mini-report. Third parties link to numbers, not to opinions.
5. **Fill the review gap.** If review sites keep showing up as sources and you have [8] reviews while competitors have [200], that's your quarter's project.

---

## Step 5: Put it on a heartbeat (this is where AI helps)

AEO isn't a one-time project. Answers change as engines and sources change.

In my clients' systems, this runs as a weekly agent workflow:

→ **Monday:** an agent reruns the 25 prompts and logs changes in mentions, position, and description.
→ **Flag:** if a description is wrong or a competitor newly appears, it posts a Slack alert with the cited sources.
→ **Suggest:** it drafts the fix (a page update, an outreach note to a listicle author, a new FAQ entry) for a human to approve.
→ **Monthly:** a one-page "AI share of answer" trend report.

The agent does the checking. A human handles the relationships and decides what to publish.

## Copy this: the Answer Audit sheet

| Column | What to log |
|---|---|
| Prompt | Buyer's question, in their words |
| Intent | Category / Problem / Comparison |
| Engine | ChatGPT / Perplexity / Gemini / Google AI |
| Mentioned | Y/N |
| Position | 1, 2, 3… |
| Description | Exact wording used about you |
| Accurate? | Y/N + what's wrong |
| Competitors | Names in the answer |
| Sources cited | URLs |
| Fix | Page / outreach / review / PR |
| Owner + date | Who, by when |

**Fair advice:** AI answers vary between sessions, so look for patterns across several runs, not single screenshots. And be wary of anyone promising "#1 in ChatGPT in 24 hours." Nobody controls the answer. You can only make yourself the most citable option.
