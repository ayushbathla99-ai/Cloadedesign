# You're Probably Prompting When You Should Be Briefing

*Why your AI marketing output is "mid," and the 6-line brief that fixes it*

---

**Stop collecting prompts.**

It's not that prompts don't matter. It's that a prompt is the *last* 10% of the instruction, and most teams are skipping the first 90%.

Last month, a [marketing lead at a B2B SaaS company] showed me their "AI content system." It was a Notion page with [60+] saved prompts.

*"Write a LinkedIn post about [topic] in a professional but engaging tone."*
*"Act as a world-class copywriter and write a landing page for…"*
*"You are an expert SEO strategist…"*

Output quality? Their words: *"Fine. Generic. We rewrite 80% of it."*

Wait, what? Sixty prompts, and they're still rewriting 80%?

Yes. Because none of those prompts told the AI anything about *their* business.

---

## Why everyone is hoarding prompts

I get it. Prompts are easy to share, easy to sell, and they *feel* like progress.

Your feed is full of them:

- "This one prompt replaced my copywriter."
- "Comment PROMPT and I'll send you my 200-prompt library."
- "The ChatGPT prompt worth $10K."

Prompts are the new growth hacks: tactics without a strategy underneath.

And they fail for the same reason growth hacks failed. **They're context-free.** A prompt that works for a cybersecurity startup and a dog-grooming app at the same time isn't working well for either.

---

## What's actually going on

Think about how you'd brief a great freelance writer.

You wouldn't say: *"Write a professional but engaging post about onboarding."*

You'd tell them:

- who it's for and what keeps those people up at night
- what you want the reader to *do* after reading
- the proof you have (customer quotes, numbers, a story)
- what you sound like, and what you never say
- what "done" looks like

That's a **brief**. And the brief is what the AI is missing.

A prompt asks for an output. A brief gives the AI the judgment to produce the *right* output.

---

## The better model: the 6-Line Brief

Every content workflow in my clients' systems starts with the same six lines. They live in a reusable context file, so the agent reads them every time without anyone retyping them.

| Line | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Objective** | What should the reader do or believe after this? | "Book a demo after realizing their onboarding leaks trials" |
| **2. Reader** | Who exactly, and what's their pain *this week*? | "Heads of Growth at 20–100-person PLG SaaS; trial-to-paid stuck under 10%" |
| **3. Evidence** | What proof can we use? | "Call quote from [customer]; activation went from X to Y" |
| **4. Angle** | What's our take that others won't say? | "Onboarding emails don't fix onboarding. The first session does." |
| **5. Guardrails** | Voice, banned words, claims we can't make | "No 'game-changer', no unverified %, British spelling" |
| **6. Done looks like** | Format, length, and a gold example | "900-word article, 3 sections, like [link to best past post]" |

Then the "prompt" becomes one line:

> *"Using the brief and the context files, write the article."*

That's it. Most of the instruction now lives in the brief, which you can reuse.

---

## How to tell if you have a prompt problem or a brief problem

Run your last five AI outputs through this test:

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Could a competitor have published this output unchanged? | Brief problem (missing Evidence + Angle) | ✓ |
| Did you rewrite the *opening* every time? | Brief problem (missing Reader) | ✓ |
| Did you rewrite the *tone* every time? | Brief problem (missing Guardrails + example) | ✓ |
| Was the output good but the *wrong thing*? | Brief problem (missing Objective) | ✓ |
| Was the output right but formatted badly? | **Actually** a prompt problem | ✓ |

In my experience, almost every team that blames their prompts has a brief problem. Only the last row is truly a prompt problem.

---

## What to do Monday morning

1. **Pick your highest-volume content type** (LinkedIn posts, case studies, emails).
2. **Write the 6-Line Brief once**, using your best-ever piece as the gold example.
3. **Save Reader, Evidence, and Guardrails as context files** your AI reads every time (a Claude Project, a custom GPT, or a folder for Claude Code).
4. **Delete half your prompt library.** Seriously.
5. **Measure one thing:** the percentage of output you rewrite. With [client], it went from [80%] to [~25%] in [3 weeks].

---

**Where I stand:** prompts aren't useless. Once the brief is solid, a sharp prompt is a nice finishing touch. But if you're spending more time collecting prompts than writing briefs, you're polishing the steering wheel of a car with no engine.

The teams winning with AI in marketing aren't the ones with the biggest prompt libraries. They're the ones with the clearest thinking, written down where the AI can read it.

**Be honest: how many saved prompts do you have, and how many written briefs?**
